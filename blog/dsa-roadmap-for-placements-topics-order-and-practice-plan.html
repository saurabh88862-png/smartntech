DSA Roadmap for Placements: Topics, Order and Practice Plan

Career• Nov 05, 2026• 9 min read

Master data structures and algorithms step by step with a realistic schedule.

Data structures and algorithms (DSA) are central to technical interviews at product companies and many service companies. The key is learning topics in the right order and practicing consistently.

Choose one language

Use the language you know best, commonly Java, C++ or Python. Learn its standard library well: in Java, that means ArrayList, HashMap, HashSet, PriorityQueue, ArrayDeque and the Collections and Arrays utility methods.

Step 1: Complexity analysis

Learn Big O notation for time and space. You must be able to explain why one solution is O(n) and another is O(n log n) or O(n squared). Interviewers always ask about this.

Step 2: Arrays and strings

  • Traversal, prefix sums and frequency counting.
  • Two pointers: pair sums, removing duplicates, reversing.
  • Sliding window: longest substring without repeating characters, maximum sum subarray of size k.
  • Sorting basics and their complexities.

Step 3: Hashing

HashMap and HashSet solve a huge range of problems: two sum, anagram grouping, first non-repeating character and subarray sum equals k.

Step 4: Recursion and backtracking

  • Base cases and recursive thinking.
  • Subsets, permutations, combination sum, N-Queens.

Recursion is the foundation for trees, graphs and dynamic programming, so do not skip it.

Step 5: Linked lists, stacks and queues

  • Reverse a linked list, detect a cycle, merge sorted lists.
  • Stacks for valid parentheses, next greater element and monotonic stack problems.
  • Queues and deques for sliding window maximum.

Step 6: Binary search

Not just on sorted arrays: binary search on the answer, such as minimum capacity to ship packages or square root, is a common interview pattern.

Step 7: Trees

  • Traversals: inorder, preorder, postorder and level order.
  • Height, diameter, lowest common ancestor.
  • Binary search tree operations and validation.

Step 8: Heaps

Top-k elements, kth largest, merging k sorted lists and finding the median of a stream.

Step 9: Graphs

  • Representation with adjacency lists.
  • BFS and DFS, connected components, cycle detection.
  • Topological sort, shortest paths with Dijkstra's algorithm, and union-find.

Step 10: Dynamic programming

Start with 1D problems (climbing stairs, house robber), then 2D (unique paths, longest common subsequence), then knapsack variations. Always write the recursive solution first, then add memoization, then convert it to a table if needed.

A realistic practice plan

  • Daily: 2 to 3 problems, about 1.5 to 2 hours.
  • Weekly: one timed contest on LeetCode or Codeforces, plus reviewing problems you could not solve.
  • Duration: about 4 to 6 months for strong placement preparation.

How to practice effectively

  • Try a problem for 20 to 30 minutes before reading hints.
  • After solving, read other solutions to learn better approaches.
  • Keep a notebook of patterns and mistakes.
  • Re-solve old problems after a week without looking at your code.
  • Explain your approach out loud, as you will in interviews.

Quality matters more than the number of problems solved. Recognizing patterns is what makes new problems feel familiar.
